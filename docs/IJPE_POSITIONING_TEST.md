# IJPE positioning test

Independent test of whether the manuscript can be framed for IJPE without
distorting its scientific contribution. The transfer-list signal is ignored
as evidence.

## Required questions

1. **Is outpatient appointment scheduling within IJPE's demonstrated
   neighborhood?** Yes, but thinly: Saremi et al. (2013, IJPE) scheduled
   outpatient *surgical* services with simulation-based optimization.
   IJPE's center of gravity is manufacturing/production systems; healthcare
   service papers exist but are occasional.

2. **Does IJPE publish healthcare/service scheduling often enough?**
   Marginal. A reviewer would not find the application exotic, but the
   paper would sit at the edge of the journal's conversation rather than
   inside it.

3. **Can the simplicity result generalize conceptually without unsupported
   claims?** Partially. The Lindley delay + waiting/idle/overtime structure
   is generic single-server scheduling, so the *concept* (one optimized
   scalar interval ≈ high-dimensional optimum) travels to job-arrival
   scheduling in production. But our evidence is computed for the
   outpatient cost model; claiming production-system results would be
   unsupported. Careful "scheduled service systems" language is defensible.

4. **Is "value of scheduling complexity" relevant to production
   economics?** Yes in principle — IJPE publishes value-of-information
   (Kaman et al. 2013; Lee 2007) and value-of-flexibility work, so
   value-of-decision-complexity is a natural IJPE-style construct. This is
   IJPE's strongest hook for this paper.

5. **Does the waiting/idle/overtime objective fit IJPE's decision
   context?** Reasonably — it is an operational resource trade-off (server
   utilization vs customer delay vs session overrun). But IJPE's stated
   theme emphasizes *economic and financial consequences*; our costs are
   weights, not monetary.

6. **Would IJPE require additional production-economic interpretation
   that would distort the paper?** Some. To satisfy "tracing economic
   consequences," the paper would need cost-parameter interpretation as
   monetary/shadow prices and a production-scheduling framing discussion.
   Doable but is reframing, not mere relabeling — moderate distortion risk.

7. **Best descriptor of the manuscript?** Computational OR (COR) is the
   most accurate descriptor. It is *not* primarily OR-methodology (EJOR
   T&M bar unmet) and *not* primarily production economics (IJPE's
   engineering-management interface with explicit economic analysis).

## Verdict: IJPE-BORDERLINE (weak)

IJPE is feasible: scope does not exclude the paper, the value-of-complexity
framing is a genuine IJPE-style contribution, and no APC is needed
(subscription route). But the production-economics center of the journal
is farther from the paper's content than COR's computational-OR center;
the economic-consequences theme would force reframing; and IJPE's high JIF
(10.0) comes with a high novelty/impact bar where "one scalar interval is
enough" — already partly anticipated by Zhou et al. 2021 — may underwhelm.
It is not favored merely because Elsevier offered a transfer.
