## Questions posed by the gate, answered

1. **Is a single-provider synthetic appointment system consequential
   enough for SEPS?** Borderline-yes: appointment interval design is a
   recurring real clinic decision; SEPS published the directly-analogous
   Chakraborty et al. 2013 slot-structure simulation. Scale is small but
   the decision class is genuinely service-sector planning.
2. **Does it optimize appointment intervals rather than
   staffing/capacity?** Yes — interval structure only; the title/abstract
   never claim capacity allocation. No overstatement found.
3. **New decision-relevant insight vs applying known tools?** Mixed —
   see the Zhou novelty boundary below; the misspecification +
   complexity-frontier + counterexample layers are not a stock exercise.
4. **Economic/social-planning interpretation grounded?** Adequately:
   cost weights are disclosed as assumptions with a sensitivity grid; no
   unsupported welfare claims.
5. **Title/abstract accurate?** Yes. Title asks the paper's actual
   question; abstract numbers all verified.
6. **Verified SEPS precedent for contribution type?** Yes — Chakraborty
   2013 (slot-structure computational evaluation) is load-bearing and
   directly analogous; 5 more verified adjacent precedents.
7. **Too long / figure-heavy (11 figs, 6 tables)?** On the heavy side for
   the finding's magnitude, but each figure carries a distinct result
   layer; SEPS has no numeric cap. Defensible; a reviewer may still ask
   to move Figs 6–8 to supplement — noted as MINOR risk, not repaired
   (retaining display items is permitted and preferred here).
8. **Reject for weak novelty despite scope fit?** THE live risk — but it
   is a *framing* risk: as written, Contributions 3–4 read as if the
   simplicity result and boundary concentration were discovered here;
   Zhou et al. 2021 already proved constant-policy near-optimality with
   finite-N bounds and documented the dome-shaped structure. An editor
   or referee who knows Zhou (likely in this field) could call the paper
   redundant *as framed*. With explicit attribution and repositioning —
   the increment is quantification under overtime, misspecification
   risk, and per-DOF frontier — the contribution is real and the paper
   is reviewable. **Simulated editorial verdict: SEND TO REVIEW after
   recalibration; without it, plausible major-revision-for-novelty or
   desk-reject for redundancy.**
