# IJPE_BRANCH_DECISION (Phase 6)

## Can the results contribute to production/service economics?

Partially and only conceptually:

- "Economic value of schedule complexity" — the value-of-complexity
  frontier IS an IJPE-style construct (value-of-information lineage:
  Kaman et al. 2013; Lee 2007 noted in prior audit).
- Opportunity cost of idle/overtime — present, but as *dimensionless
  weights* in a synthetic objective, not monetary costs.
- Capacity planning under uncertainty — present topically.

## What IJPE explicitly expects that we lack

- "Essential novel scientific contribution": every verified relevant IJPE
  precedent proposes a new model, algorithm, or analytical result (DRO
  newsvendor 2022 = new models + real data; surgery scheduling 2019–2020
  = new algorithms; platform/queueing papers = new equilibrium analyses).
  Our paper deliberately uses established methods — the novelty is the
  question and the computational evidence. That is precisely what COR
  rejected.
- "Tracing economic and financial consequences … central theme": our
  objective is a weighted waiting/idle/overtime index. Relabeling it as
  economic impact is prohibited (prompt forbids it, and it would be
  inaccurate). Honest framing can call it a proxy cost, but that weakens
  the central-theme fit.
- Production-systems generality: our evidence is outpatient-specific; the
  positioning test already flagged that claiming production results
  would be unsupported.

## Counter-evidence in IJPE's favor

- Saremi et al. 2013: outpatient appointment scheduling has appeared in
  IJPE (13 years old — weak precedent strength today).
- Stylized service models with managerial insight do appear (Chen et al.
  2021/2022; Legros 2021; Wang et al. 2024) — but each contributes a new
  stylized model.
- JIF 10.0, Q1, no-APC route: eligible, but JIF is not a fit argument
  (decision rule: precedent > JIF).

## Verdict: NO-GO (weak CONDITIONAL at best)

The match fails on the journal's *own stated bar*, not on scope alone: no
new construct, no economic calibration, production center far from the
application. A feasible path exists only via genuine new work — e.g., a
formal bound on the complexity frontier or a real cost calibration —
which is exactly the kind of new research the previous gate declined for
EJOR. Submitting as-is would most likely reproduce the COR outcome
(insufficient-contribution rejection), now with an added scope objection
(healthcare OR at the edge of production economics).

If ever pursued: would require (a) an economic-consequences re-derivation
(shadow prices/monetary calibration), and (b) ideally one new formal or
real-data element. That is new research, outside this gate's mandate.
