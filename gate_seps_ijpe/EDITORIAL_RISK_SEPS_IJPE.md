# EDITORIAL_RISK_SEPS_IJPE (Phase 7 — desk-rejection red-team)

Informed by two actual desk outcomes: Omega (scope mismatch — "closer to
the cited literature") and COR (insufficient constructive-algorithmic /
computer-OR contribution). Simulated as each journal's handling editor.

## SEPS editor simulation

- **Strongest scope-reject rationale:** "A single-physician synthetic
  clinic is an OR-methods exercise, not socio-economic planning." The
  journal's criterion permits uniqueness in method OR application OR
  context — our application is known, our method is standard, so the
  editor must accept the *question itself* (value of complexity under
  distributional uncertainty) as the unique element. Risk: **SCOPE,
  moderate.** Mitigable in the cover letter + intro by anchoring the
  decision context (clinic capacity planning) and citing the SEPS slot-
  structure precedent.
- **Contribution-type rationale:** "We already know simple rules work;
  what does a synthetic design-space sweep add?" Answer is the
  quantified frontier + misspecification penalty + counterexamples —
  a when-complexity-pays map, which is a planning insight, not a method.
  Risk: **NOVELTY, low-moderate** (the simplicity result is already
  partly anticipated by Zhou et al. 2021 asymptotics — must be
  positioned as finite-horizon quantification + frontier, not discovery
  of simplicity).
- **Evidence/validation objection:** "No real data." SEPS tolerates
  stylized models (queueing-planning papers, simulation capacity
  studies) but recent norm leans case-anchored. Risk: **EVIDENCE,
  moderate.** Resolved by honest framing + possibly citing that the
  distributional descriptors (CV, tail ratio) are measurable from any
  clinic's own data — an implementable rule, not an unvalidated claim.
- **Citation ecology weakness:** zero current SEPS refs — fixable with
  the 3–5 semantic additions in `CITATION_ECOLOGY_SEPS_IJPE.csv`.
- **Does manuscript-specific adjustment resolve it?** Yes for
  scope/contribution framing; the evidence objection is mitigated, not
  eliminated. No new data/analysis required.
- **Would genuinely new research be needed?** No.

## IJPE editor simulation

- **Strongest scope-reject rationale:** "Healthcare appointment
  scheduling without economic consequence is outside the engineering–
  management/production core; Saremi 2013 is the exception, not the
  conversation." Risk: **SCOPE, moderate-high.**
- **Contribution-type rationale:** "No new model, algorithm, or bound —
  a simulation sweep with standard SAA. Where is the essential novel
  scientific contribution?" This is the COR letter again, plus a higher
  bar (JIF-10 journal). Risk: **NOVELTY, high — the dominant failure
  mode.**
- **Evidence/validation objection:** "Weighted costs are not economic
  consequences; no calibration, no industrial setting." Risk:
  **EVIDENCE, moderate-high** — the journal's own wording makes this a
  stated expectation, not a reviewer whim.
- **Citation ecology weakness:** zero IJPE refs; the VOI/production-econ
  neighborhood is foreign to the bibliography — additions are justifiable
  but thinner and more visible as effort.
- **Does manuscript-specific adjustment resolve it?** Only partially —
  reframing can soften scope, but cannot manufacture the missing novel
  construct or economic calibration.
- **Would genuinely new research be needed?** **Yes** (a formal
  bound/theorem or a real cost calibration). Outside current mandate.
