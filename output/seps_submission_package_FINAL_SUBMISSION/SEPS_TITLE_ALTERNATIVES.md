# SEPS title alternatives (Phase 13)

Chosen: **"When Does Appointment-Scheduling Complexity Pay? Service-Time
Uncertainty and the Value of Simple Planning Rules"**

Rationale: names the planning question and the paper's actual result type
(value of simple rules) without hiding appointment scheduling or claiming
capacity optimization; mirrors the SEPS slot-structure conversation
(Chakraborty et al. 2013).

Alternatives considered:
1. "When Does Scheduling Complexity Pay? Appointment Scheduling under
   Service-Time Distributional Uncertainty" — the COR title; accurate but
   reads methods-first rather than planning-first. Rejected (residual COR
   framing).
2. "The Value of a Common Interval: Appointment-Slot Design under
   Service-Time Distributional Uncertainty" — clean but foregrounds the
   answer over the decision question; slightly overstates (result is
   conditional on studied scenarios). Rejected.
3. "How Much Scheduling Complexity Should a Clinic Buy? Finite-Session
   Evidence on Appointment Intervals" — vivid planning framing but
   informal and rhetorical. Rejected.
4. "Service-Time Shape and the Marginal Value of Schedule Complexity in
   Outpatient Appointment Systems" — accurate, but buries the planning
   decision. Rejected.
5. "Simple Rules Suffice? Distributional Misspecification and the Value of
   Appointment-Scheduling Complexity" — catchy but the leading claim needs
   the question mark and underplays misspecification. Rejected.
6. Chosen title (above) — prompt's candidate direction, verified against
   the contribution map: "complexity pay" = the C_K* frontier; "simple
   planning rules" = the uniform-interval result; "service-time
   uncertainty" = the distributional design. No overclaim.
